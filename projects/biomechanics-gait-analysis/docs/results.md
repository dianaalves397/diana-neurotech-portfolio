# Results and interpretation

## Knee flexion

Mean knee flexion increased as locomotion became faster. The right knee showed the higher mean value in every condition.

| Condition | Left mean | Right mean | Left max | Right max | Symmetry index |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Walking | 17.8° | 23.6° | 57.4° | 63.6° | 28.4% |
| Running | 36.4° | 43.0° | 76.7° | 89.5° | 16.7% |
| Sprint | 43.6° | 50.3° | 103.1° | 113.1° | 14.3% |

The symmetry index decreased from walking to running and sprint, indicating greater right-left similarity in the mean knee-flexion metric at the faster conditions, despite the right side remaining higher.

## Contact and no-contact timing

Vertical ground-reaction force was used with a 50 N threshold to estimate support periods.

| Condition | Support L | Support R | No-contact L | No-contact R | Stride time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Walking | 0.77 s | 0.77 s | 0.51 s | 0.50 s | ~1.27 s |
| Running | 0.28 s | 0.26 s | 0.41 s | 0.42 s | ~0.69 s |
| Sprint | 0.23 s | 0.21 s | 0.41 s | 0.42 s | ~0.63–0.64 s |

Support time fell strongly with increasing speed. Walking remained close to the expected support-dominant gait pattern, while running and sprint showed progressively larger no-contact fractions.

## Step and stride timing

The reported mean step time decreased from approximately **0.63 s** in walking to **0.34 s** in running and **0.32 s** in sprint. Mean stride time decreased from about **1.27 s** to **0.69 s** and then **0.63 s**.

## Spatial estimates

Step and stride lengths were estimated from lateral-malleolus trajectories:

| Condition | Mean step length | Mean stride length |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Walking | ~0.32 m | ~0.65 m |
| Running | ~0.38 m | ~0.75 m |
| Sprint | ~0.22 m | ~0.43 m |

These are approximations based on the available marker trajectories rather than direct foot markers.

## Marker trajectories

The distal **ML (lateral malleolus)** marker showed the largest displacement. Vertical amplitude increased markedly with speed:

- walking: **150.0–202.5 mm**;
- running: **254.8–287.2 mm**;
- sprint: **455.3–478.2 mm**.

The **GT (greater trochanter)** was comparatively stable, with vertical amplitudes ranging from approximately **29.1 to 99.4 mm** across conditions.

## Experimental interpretation

The combined timing and kinematic results show the transition from a support-dominant walking pattern toward faster locomotion characterised by shorter ground-contact time, larger no-contact phases, greater knee flexion and larger distal-segment excursion.

One isolated right-ML acceleration value during walking was substantially larger than the corresponding left-side value. It was treated as a likely measurement artefact or marker/capture disturbance during interpretation rather than as a representative biomechanical trend.
